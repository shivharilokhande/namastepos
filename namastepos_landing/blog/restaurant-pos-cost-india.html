Restaurant POS Cost in India: What You Actually Pay in Year One

Updated 4 Sep 2026·9 min read·Buyer's Guide

Short answer: your year-one POS cost is not the number on the quote. It is software plus hardware plus setup plus the things billed separately — reports, extra outlets, extra staff logins, support visits, an annual maintenance contract — plus, on some models, a percentage of every order. Which of those lines you pay depends on the pricing model you choose, not the feature list. There are three models in the Indian market, and they land in very different places by month twelve.

The four cost lines nobody puts on the quote

Ask any owner who has changed systems and they'll tell you the same thing: the surprise was never the licence fee. It was everything around it.

  1. Setup and menu entry. Someone has to key in 200 items with prices, variants, modifiers and tax rates. If the vendor does it, it is often a one-time fee. If you do it, it costs you two evenings.
  2. Annual maintenance contract. A separate yearly charge on many desktop systems, sometimes framed as compulsory to keep getting updates and support.
  3. Per-feature charges. Reports and exports. Each extra outlet. Each extra staff login. GST or e-invoice modules. A separate charge for the KOT printer module. Each one is small; together they are frequently larger than the base fee.
  4. Support. Ask whether a visit is included or billed. Ask what the response time is on a Saturday night.

None of this is dishonest. It is just how the quote is constructed. Your job as the buyer is to force all of it onto one sheet of paper before you sign.

The one that catches people out is column two. "Free software" with a commission is not free — it is a variable price that rises exactly as your business succeeds.

Do the commission maths once

Take your own monthly sales and multiply. If a tool takes 3% and you do ₹5,00,000 a month, that is ₹15,000 a month, or ₹1,80,000 a year. At 2% on ₹3,00,000 a month it is ₹6,000 a month, ₹72,000 a year. Do it with your real numbers before you compare anything to a fixed monthly fee, because a fixed fee that looks expensive next to "free" usually stops looking expensive after this one calculation.

The same arithmetic applies in reverse to annual-upfront licences. A figure paid in April is real money out of working capital in April — the month you probably needed it for stock and salaries. Monthly billing is not just cheaper on paper; it is gentler on cash flow, and it keeps the vendor honest, because they have to earn next month.

Hardware: what you actually need

Be ruthless here. The classic counter setup — a desktop PC, a monitor, a UPS, a cash drawer, a barcode scanner, a dedicated KOT printer — exists because the software required it. If your POS runs on Android, iOS and the web, the phone in your pocket is the till, and the list shrinks to one item you might genuinely want: a thermal receipt printer, so guests get a printed bill and the kitchen gets a KOT on paper.

We are not going to invent prices for you, because printer and drawer prices vary by city, model and how well you negotiate. Do this instead: get two local quotes for a Bluetooth or LAN thermal printer, and compare them to whatever the software vendor wants to sell you bundled. Buying hardware from the software company is convenient and almost never the cheapest line on the sheet.

Six questions that reveal the real price

  1. What is the all-in figure for twelve months, including setup, AMC and hardware, in writing?
  2. What is the renewal price in year two?
  3. Which features are charged separately — reports, exports, GST, e-invoice, extra outlets, extra logins?
  4. If I stop paying in month four, what happens to my data, and can I export it?
  5. Is support included, and what happens at 9pm on a Saturday?
  6. Can I take a full order, fire a KOT and print a bill with the internet switched off? Show me.

Cheap is not the goal. Predictable is. Frequently asked questions

How much does a restaurant POS cost per month in India?
It depends entirely on the pricing model rather than the feature list. A monthly all-inclusive SaaS POS is a fixed per-register fee you can stop paying. NamastePOS is ₹0 on Starter, ₹299 per month or ₹2,990 per year on Growth, ₹799 or ₹7,990 on Pro, ₹999 or ₹9,990 on Advanced, and ₹1,999 or ₹19,990 on Enterprise — all per register. A legacy desktop POS is usually quoted as one annual figure paid upfront, and a commission-based ordering tool looks free until you multiply the percentage by your monthly sales.
What hidden costs should I ask about before buying a POS?
Ask five questions in writing. Is there a one-time setup, onboarding or menu-entry fee? Is there an annual maintenance contract, and what does it cover? Are reports, exports, extra outlets, extra staff logins or GST features charged separately? Is support included or billed per visit? And what is the price at renewal, not just in year one? The gap between the quoted number and the year-one number is almost always in those five answers.
Is a free restaurant POS actually free?
A genuinely free plan is free but limited, and that is fine as a starting point. Our Starter plan is ₹0 forever with a single counter and core billing, KOT, QR ordering and offline mode. The version to be careful with is free-plus-commission, where the software costs nothing and a percentage of every order goes out instead — that is not free, it is a variable price that grows with your success.
Do I need to buy hardware for a restaurant POS?
Not necessarily. If your POS runs on a phone, the phone in your pocket is the till, and the only real purchase is a thermal receipt printer if you want printed bills and KOTs. A desktop POS is the expensive path because it needs a PC or terminal at the counter, a UPS, and a replacement when the machine fails. Get two local quotes for the printer before you buy from the software vendor.
