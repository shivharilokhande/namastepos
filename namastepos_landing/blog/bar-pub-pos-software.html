Bar & Pub POS Software in India: Billing, Stock & Tabs

Updated 28 Aug 2026·8 min read·Buyer's guide

Bars are fast, cash-heavy, and unusually exposed to shrinkage. A round has to be rung in seconds, tabs stay open for hours, and expensive liquor walks out the door if nobody's counting. A good bar POS is built around speed, tabs and tight stock control — here's what to look for.

Why bars need a different POS

A bar's economics hinge on two things a restaurant worries about less: speed of service during a rush and control of high-value stock. Drinks are ordered in quick rounds, often on an open tab, and the product is liquid, portable and pricey — the perfect conditions for shrinkage if the system is loose. So while a bar shares plenty with a restaurant POS, its make-or-break features are running tabs, peg-level stock, flexible pricing, and airtight void/discount controls.

Running tabs done right

Guests don't settle after every drink; they run a tab. Your POS should keep one open bill per table or guest, let staff keep adding rounds all evening, and then settle cleanly at the end — with split payments when a group divides the bill. If the system forces a fresh transaction per round, or can't merge an evening's rounds into one clear total, service slows and mistakes creep in. A proper running-bill flow, with multi-round support, is the backbone of bar billing.

Liquor inventory: track to the peg

General food inventory isn't enough for a bar. You need stock tracked at the level you actually sell — pegs and bottles — so every pour draws down stock and you can compare expected usage to a physical bottle count. That variance is your theft-and-spillage number, and in a bar it's often where the real money leaks. When a 750ml bottle should yield a known number of pegs and consistently yields fewer, the POS is telling you something you need to know.

Happy hours and flexible pricing

Bars change prices constantly — happy-hour rates, pitcher vs glass, premium vs house pour. Set these up as variants and pricing rules once, and staff simply pick the right option instead of doing mental maths at a crowded counter. Consistent, correct pricing during the busiest, noisiest hours protects both your margin and your customers' trust.

Closing the theft door

Shrinkage in bars usually hides in three places: unrecorded pours, "friendly" comped drinks, and voids after a round is served. The fix isn't suspicion — it's structure. Require a reason and a staff login for every void and discount, log them, and review the patterns. Combined with peg-level stock variance, you get a clear picture of where product is going. Most staff are honest; a system that removes ambiguity keeps it that way and quickly surfaces the exceptions.

Speed is a feature

On a busy Saturday, seconds per round add up to a shorter or longer queue at the bar. A mobile-first, offline-first POS that rings a round in a few taps — without waiting on a server for each action — is worth real money in throughput. And because it keeps working through an outage, a dropped connection never turns your busiest night into chaos.

Compliance and the bill

Bars still owe correct, GST-compliant tax invoices, and liquor can carry different tax treatment from food — so your POS needs to apply the right rates and produce a clean, itemised bill and GST-ready reports. Handle the compliance automatically at billing time and month-end filing becomes review-and-submit, not a scramble. As always, confirm the specifics of liquor taxation for your state and licence with your CA.

Frequently asked questions

What features does a bar POS need?
Fast round entry, running tabs per table/guest, peg/bottle liquor stock, happy-hour and variant pricing, strong void/discount controls, and quick split payments.
How do bars control liquor theft?
Tie every pour to a sale, track stock to the peg, compare expected vs actual bottle counts, and log every void/discount to a staff member. A POS that does both makes theft visible.
Can a bar run tabs on a POS?
Yes — a running tab adds rounds to one open bill per table/guest and settles at the end, with split payments. NamastePOS supports running bills, multiple rounds and split payments.
Does a bar POS work offline?
Yes — NamastePOS rings rounds and prints bills offline and syncs later, so a busy night never stops.
