Free Restaurant Billing Software in India: What's the Catch?

Updated 28 Aug 2026·8 min read·Buyer's guide

“Free” ranges from genuinely useful to a bait-and-switch. If you're opening or running a small restaurant, a good free plan can cover your real needs for a long time. Here's how to tell a fair free tier from a trap, and what to expect as you grow.

Can a restaurant really run on free software?

Yes — for a small or new outlet, a well-designed free plan can handle day-to-day billing, kitchen tickets, a menu and basic reports without costing a rupee. The key is that the free tier includes the essentials, not a crippled demo. The catch to watch for isn't that free exists; it's what's missing and what happens when you outgrow it.

What a fair free plan should include

  • GST-compliant billing — proper tax invoices, not a toy receipt.
  • KOT / kitchen tickets — orders reaching the kitchen cleanly.
  • A real menu — categories, variants and modifiers.
  • Basic reports — daily sales and best-sellers.
  • Offline billing — it shouldn't stop when the internet does.
  • Your data, exportable — no holding your sales hostage.

Where free plans are (fairly) limited

Free tiers usually cap the things that correlate with a bigger business: number of staff logins, number of outlets, menu-item count, advanced inventory, deeper analytics, integrations, or add-ons like e-invoicing at scale. That's reasonable — you pay when you're big enough to need more. The unfair versions instead limit the basics (a handful of bills a day, no GST invoice) to force an upgrade before you've even validated your business.

The real questions to ask

  1. Are the essentials free — GST bills, KOT, offline — or paywalled?
  2. What exactly triggers a paid upgrade, and is the pricing published?
  3. Can I export my data and leave if I want to?
  4. Does it scale to more staff and outlets without a migration?

Free as a starting point, not a ceiling

The best way to think about free software is as a low-risk start. You get running with zero upfront cost, prove your restaurant works, and upgrade deliberately when you genuinely need more staff logins, outlets or advanced features — on pricing you can see in advance. Avoid anything that makes the basics painful or your data hard to take with you; a fair free plan should earn your paid business by being good, not by trapping you.
