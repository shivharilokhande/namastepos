Table Management & Reservations: Turn Tables Faster

Updated 28 Aug 2026·8 min read·Operations

On a busy night, your revenue ceiling is set by how many times you can turn a table. Good table management and reservations don't just look organised — they directly lift covers, cut waits, and keep the kitchen from being ambushed.

Why table turns are the real revenue lever

A restaurant has a fixed number of seats and a fixed number of prime hours. The only way to serve more guests in that window is to turn tables faster and waste fewer of them. Table management is the discipline of knowing, at a glance, which tables are free, seated, waiting to order, waiting for the bill, or ready to clear — so nothing sits idle and nobody is forgotten.

A live floor view

The core feature is a floor map that shows status in real time. Instead of a host walking the room to check, the whole team sees which tables are open and which are lingering on a paid bill and can be reset. Multiply the seconds saved across a Friday night and you've added covers without adding seats.

Running bills and multi-course KOTs

Dine-in orders arrive in rounds — starters, mains, dessert. Your system should fire each round to the kitchen as its own KOT while keeping one running bill for the table, so the guest gets one clean total and the kitchen gets each course on time. If your POS forces you to close and reopen a table per round, it's fighting how dining actually works and slowing your floor.

Guest count, joins and splits

Real tables merge and divide. Two friends join a four-top; a big group splits the bill four ways. Table management should let you set guest counts (useful for pacing and per-cover analytics), join tables into one bill, and split payments at the end without a spreadsheet. These small conveniences are what keep service smooth when the room is full.

Reservations that prevent, not create, chaos

Reservations are a promise you have to keep. A simple booking view that blocks double-bookings, holds tables for the right window, and shows the host tonight's arrivals turns reservations from a paper diary risk into a smooth seating plan. The aim isn't a complex booking engine — it's never seating two parties on the same table.

What to measure

Once tables are managed, watch average turn time and covers per shift. If turns are slow, look at where tables stall — ordering, kitchen, or the bill — and fix that step. Faster, calmer service and higher covers per night are the payoff, and they show up directly in your sales.
