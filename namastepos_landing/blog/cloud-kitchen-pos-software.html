POS Software for Cloud Kitchens in India: What to Look For

Updated 28 Aug 2026·9 min read·Buyer's guide

A cloud kitchen is a different animal from a dine-in restaurant. There's no floor, no table service, and often several brands cooking out of one kitchen. Your POS priorities shift accordingly — toward order speed, food-cost precision, and clean per-brand numbers. Here's what to look for.

Why a cloud kitchen needs a different POS

In a dine-in restaurant, half your POS value is table and floor management. A cloud (or "dark") kitchen has none of that. Instead, almost every order arrives through a delivery channel or a pickup counter, margins are thin, and speed in the kitchen is everything. So the features that matter most are the ones that keep orders flowing cleanly to the line and keep food cost under a microscope. Paying for a heavy dine-in feature set you'll never touch is the classic cloud-kitchen mistake.

The must-have checklist

  • Multi-channel order capture — orders from delivery aggregators, your own site/app and phone, all landing in one place.
  • Kitchen Display System (KDS) — the heart of a cloud kitchen; live tickets, timers, station routing.
  • Recipe-linked inventory — every sale draws down ingredients so you see real food cost, not a guess.
  • Multi-brand support — run several virtual brands from one kitchen, each with its own menu and P&L.
  • Per-channel & per-brand reporting — know which brand and which channel actually make money after commissions.
  • GST-compliant billing — tax invoices and GSTR-ready exports, even for delivery-only.
  • Offline-first — a kitchen can't stop taking orders because the Wi-Fi dropped.

Running multiple brands from one kitchen

The economics of cloud kitchens push almost everyone toward multiple virtual brands — the same kitchen and staff producing for "Biryani House", "Wrap Co." and "Dessert Lab" simultaneously. Your POS should make that painless: separate menus and separate reporting per brand, but a single consolidated order flow so the kitchen isn't juggling three systems. When you can see each brand's revenue, food cost and margin separately, you can double down on winners and quietly retire the ones that don't work.

Food cost is the whole business

With no ambience to sell and aggregator commissions taking a bite, a cloud kitchen's profit lives entirely in food cost and throughput. That makes recipe-linked inventory non-negotiable: when each dish knows its ingredients, every order updates stock automatically, and you can compare theoretical usage against actual to catch spoilage and over-portioning early. A dish that looks popular but quietly loses money after commission and ingredient cost is exactly the thing you need your POS to expose.

Aggregator orders: keep them in one flow

Most cloud-kitchen orders arrive from delivery platforms. The operational risk is fragmentation — staff tabbing between separate tablets, re-keying orders, missing tickets during a rush. Whatever your channel mix, the goal is a single kitchen queue where every order, wherever it came from, appears the same way to the cooks. Consolidated capture also means your reports finally tell the truth about which channel is worth the commission and which isn't.

Speed and reliability beat bells and whistles

When you evaluate a cloud-kitchen POS, resist the feature checklist arms race. What actually moves your numbers is: how fast an order reaches the line, whether the kitchen can see wait times, whether food cost is visible in real time, and whether it all keeps working when the internet doesn't. Get those right and you have a kitchen that ships hot food fast at a known cost. Everything else is secondary.

Start lean, scale by brand

You don't need an enterprise deployment to open a cloud kitchen. Start with one brand on a free or low-cost plan, get the KDS and inventory flow tight, then add brands as you prove demand. A POS that lets you begin small and grow brand-by-brand — without a rip-and-replace when you scale — is worth more than one that demands a big commitment on day one.
